Arc mainnet is chain `5042`. USDC on it implements EIP-3009 and is allowlisted here, so mainnet settlement works — the same contract at the same address as on testnet, with the same EIP-712 domain. Nothing about your integration changes except the network identifier.

## Before you point anything at it

Mainnet moves real USDC, so check that this instance will actually settle. Mainnet settlement is behind a switch (`MAINNET_ENABLED`) that ships **off**:

<NetCode lang="bash">
{`curl -s {{facilitatorUrl}}/health | jq '.networks[] | select(.network=="mainnet")'`}
</NetCode>

- `status: "disabled"` — `/verify` works, `/settle` returns `503 mainnet_disabled`. You can validate an integration end to end without anyone spending anything.
- `status: "ok"` — settlement is live, and the `balanceUsdc` and `estimatedSettlesLeft` fields tell you how much runway the signer has.
- `status: "degraded"` — the RPC is unreachable or the signer is below its operating floor. See [limits](/docs/limits).

If you need mainnet reliably on, with your own signer and your own limits, [run your own instance](/docs/self-host). It is the same code and one environment variable.

## Switching your config

One string:

<NetCode lang="ts">
{`accepts: {
  scheme: "exact",
  network: "{{caip2}}",      // was eip155:5042002
  payTo: "0xYourMerchantAddress",
  price: "$0.01",
}`}
</NetCode>

The asset address is the same on both networks, so there is nothing else to change. The facilitator URL stays the same too — x402 carries the network inside the payment payload.

> Use a different `payTo` for mainnet than for testnet. The settlement log is public, and tying test traffic to your production revenue address makes both easier to analyse than you probably want.

## Getting USDC onto Arc

- [Circle's USDC Bridge](https://bridge.usdc.com) moves USDC directly between supported chains.
- CCTP burns and mints natively rather than wrapping; Arc's CCTP domain is `26`. Addresses are in the [network reference](/docs/network-reference).
- Gateway gives you a chain-abstracted USDC balance you can draw on from Arc.

Remember that USDC on Arc is the gas token, so funding a wallet for gas and funding it to spend are the same action.

## Running your own signer on mainnet

If you self-host, two things are worth getting right before you enable mainnet:

1. **Fund the signer and watch it.** Gas comes out of its USDC balance. `MIN_SIGNER_BALANCE_USDC` makes `/settle` refuse cleanly rather than broadcast into failure — which matters on Arc, where an underfunded or underpriced transaction is silently dropped by the mempool instead of reverting.
2. **Run `pnpm verify-assets` against mainnet.** It reads the token's real `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR()` and proves the registry's EIP-712 domain against it. A wrong domain fails every signature with nothing on-chain to debug, and that is a far more annoying thing to discover in production than in a script.

Then [self-host](/docs/self-host) has the rest of the runbook.
